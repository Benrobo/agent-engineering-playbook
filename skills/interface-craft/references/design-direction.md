# Choosing a visual direction

This is a project design method, separate from the source cheat sheet.

## Make the direction specific enough to guide decisions

Start from the product's actual use. A reflective reading companion, a research workspace, and a publishing tool need different rhythms even if all three use the same component library.

Complete this short brief:

- **Task:** What should someone accomplish on this screen?
- **Attention:** What should they notice first, second, and only when needed?
- **Character:** Choose two compatible qualities and explain what they mean visually.
- **Composition:** Decide where the main content, navigation, supporting detail, and action belong.
- **Signature:** Choose one recognizable treatment rooted in the product.
- **Restraint:** Identify what would distract from the task and leave it out.

“Clean and modern” is not a sufficient brief. “A calm reading surface with marginal annotations and a restrained progress marker” leads to decisions you can evaluate.

## Build distinction from the domain

Use the shape of the content to find an organizing idea. A research product could make evidence trails a first-class visual element. A publishing product could put a realistic content preview at the center of the workflow. A reading product could use annotations, chapters, and returning-to-place behavior as its identity.

Those are examples, not templates to stamp onto every project. Choose an approach that makes the product easier to understand. A signature detail should survive removing decorative assets and should not obstruct a familiar interaction.

Treat typography, density, composition, illustration, and interaction as coordinated choices. Do not make every layer compete for attention. A restrained system can be distinctive through proportions and content; a more expressive system still needs a clear task hierarchy.

## Work inside existing products

Preserve recognizable navigation and established meaning. A small account-settings change rarely warrants new fonts, a global palette migration, or a different component library. Carry the product's character into the new feature through its existing vocabulary.

When redesign is requested, establish the scope: one component, one flow, or the whole product. Avoid an attractive isolated screen that cannot coexist with the rest of the application.

## Content is a design input

Write realistic labels and sample records before finalizing the layout. Include very long names, missing images, older timestamps, larger numeric values, and different permissions. Distinguish an empty account from a filter returning no matches. Explain actual outcomes rather than adding vague marketing language to product controls.

Do not invent customer logos, testimonials, adoption counts, or compliance claims to make a mockup feel finished. Use clearly identified sample data when real content is unavailable.

## Decision check

Compare the rendered result with the brief. Can a new visitor identify the next useful action? Does the visual signature belong to this product? Can someone perform the task without noticing the decorative work? Remove anything that weakens those answers.
